# Draft 2.0

Date: October 31, 2026
Status: Direction agreed with Erik on October 31, 2026. Supersedes the October 24 writing baseline. The personal-update system (Features page, official updates, Restart to update) carries forward unchanged.

## Purpose

Draft is an always-running Mac app for writing, iterating on, and publishing drafts for social platforms. Everything you add is a draft. A draft holds every version you've written, its images and videos, the links it references, and where it was published and how it did.

Draft 2.0 starts from the current app and keeps its local document store, Markdown editor, image paste and social cropping, connected Claude Code/Codex providers, and the ability for anyone to ship their own features into their copy of the app. Writing tools that don't serve the new direction are removed.

## Language

- The app is **Draft**. The things you write are **drafts**, never "notes" or "documents" in the interface.
- **Version**: one column of a draft, numbered v1, v2, v3…
- **Reference**: a link, image, or video that appears in any draft. References are a view across drafts, not a separate kind of item.

## Always available

- Draft runs at login and stays running. Closing the window hides it; quitting is explicit.
- An optional menu bar icon (on by default, can be turned off in Preferences) opens Draft and offers New draft and Quit.
- **⌥.** from anywhere brings the full Draft window to the front on the current desktop with a new blank draft. If the most recent draft is still empty, reuse it instead of creating another. Pressing ⌥. while you are already in Draft hides it and returns you to the app you were using.
- **⌘+ / ⌘−** zoom the whole window (⌘= works as ⌘+), **⌘0** resets, and the zoom level is kept between launches.
- Each draft and version keeps its scroll position, so switching away and back returns you to the same place.
- **⌘N** inside Draft opens a new blank draft, with the same empty-draft reuse.
- All drafts save continuously. Nothing is lost by opening a new draft, hiding the window, or quitting.

## Writing

- Markdown by default: writing, editing, pasting, and formatting all produce Markdown. Pasted rich text becomes Markdown.
- Paste or drop an image to store it in the draft. Crop it to a social aspect ratio at any time; the original stays recoverable.
- Paste or drop a video file (mp4, mov, webm) to store it in the draft and play it inline.
- A pasted video or web link shows as a rich preview (title, thumbnail, source) inside the draft.
- **Drag blocks.** Paragraphs, headings, lists, images, videos, and link previews can be dragged to a new position. While dragging, a drop indicator shows exactly where the block will land. Dropping is one undoable action.
- An empty draft shows only a blinking caret (no placeholder text). The caret is the height of the text, also on empty lines. The Mac draws the highlight, so it always fills its line evenly above and below the text, whether it is one word or a whole line. While dragging selected text, a blinking cursor shows where it will land.
- Each version's tab ends with its number, its short reading time and its characters, as "2m · 1,840 char" (seconds under a minute, as in "12s", then "4m" and "1h"; about 238 words per minute). Highlighting text switches the time and characters to the selection, as "3s · 62 char".
- Draft never shows a scrollbar; everything still scrolls with the trackpad, mouse wheel and keyboard.
- The whole window is frosted glass: the Mac blurs whatever is behind it, and one light (or dark) tint covers the app. The lines between columns are thinner places in the tint, so more of what is behind the window shows through. The tabs, the open draft in the sidebar (with its versions) and the Settings button are frosted panes laid on the glass, with a soft wash and a blur of their own, so text scrolling under them is softened instead of showing straight through; the tab you are in and the open draft in the sidebar have the same, strongest wash, a hovered tab a little less, the others least, and each tab's glass is one continuous shape, so there is no line where a neck meets a bubble. Renaming a version edits its name in place inside the tab, with no focus ring or box.
- The window never scrolls or bounces: the sidebar, its top strip, its footer and the version tabs always stay in place. Only the draft list, each version's text and Preferences scroll, and they stop at their edges.
- The window has no visible title bar: the version tabs sit at the very top beside the window buttons, which float over an empty strip at the top of the sidebar. Dragging the tab row (tabs included) or that strip moves the window; double-clicking zooms it. The sidebar has no status filters for now.
- There is no top bar: no logo, title, sidebar button, export or new-draft button. The sidebar and the version tabs start at the top of the window. Settings is a round glass gear in the lower left corner of the sidebar, floating over the draft list like the version tabs float over the text (it gets a dot when an update is waiting); with the sidebar hidden, use ⌘,. Settings looks like writing: the sidebar lists its sections (Make it yours, Your changes, Official features, Updates, Appearance, Mac, Shortcuts, AI, Files, Sync), each with a line about it and a count or value on the right. Clicking one shows it in place of the last, one page at a time under a single glass tab with its name. The section you are in is highlighted in the sidebar like the open draft. ⌃Tab and ⌃⇧Tab step through the sections, ⌘W goes back to writing, ⌘B hides the sidebar, and the last section you viewed is remembered. Appearance is Light, Dark or System (following your Mac, the default). The gear, highlighted while Settings is open, goes back to writing, and the Draft menu has Settings… ⌘,. ⌘N makes a new draft, ⌘B shows and hides the sidebar, Export is in the sidebar's right-click menu, and drafts always save, so only a failure is shown.
- **⌃Tab** and **⌃⇧Tab** move between the open version columns.

## Titles

1. A new draft is titled by its first sentence (trimmed to a sensible length).
2. Once there is enough text (about 50 words), the connected AI suggests a better title, which replaces the first-sentence title.
3. Renaming a draft makes its title manual. A manual title is never replaced automatically.
4. Without a connected AI, the first-sentence title stays.

## Versions

- Each version has its own undo and redo history. ⌘Z and ⇧⌘Z act on the version you are typing in, histories survive switching drafts and restarting Draft, and they are stored in the draft's package.
- Versions open as glass tabs laid side by side, oldest on the left. Each version has its own title: the name you give it with Rename, or else its own first sentence (never the AI title), which names its tab on the left and its row in the sidebar's version list; an empty version reads "Untitled". On the far right of the tab are its reading time and characters. Version numbers are never shown: tabs, the sidebar's version list and menus show only each version's name. The tabs float over the top of their columns with nothing behind them: the text scrolls up behind the frosted glass and fades out at the window's top edge, and the caret never hides under a tab. Each tab is a row of separate glass bubbles rounded 8 points with no outline, joined by short necks scooped from above and below, with no seam where they meet (the same shape and rounding as the Studio Four sidebar's nav pills) at the left of its column: the name (cut off with "…" past 260 points, so a single open version never becomes one long bar; hovering a cut-off title scrolls it on a loop to show all of it, as in the sidebar), the reading time and the characters. A thin line divides the sidebar from the first column and each column from the next. It runs the full height of the window, with the tab set 8px in from it (the same space the sidebar items have from the sidebar's edges), is a thinner place in the tint rather than a painted line, and keeps its look while you switch, hover or resize. With the sidebar closed, the first column has no line. The tab you are in is the whitest. Tabs have no close button: close one with ⌘W or its right-click menu. Pressing a tab switches to that version, and dragging it moves the window. There is no other toolbar in the writing area.
- **⌘W** closes the version tab you are in (the draft keeps the version; the last open tab stays). Closing the window is ⇧⌘W, so ⌘W never hides Draft.
- Versions open side by side. **⌘T** opens a copy of the version you are in, in a new column beside it. The column you are typing in is the active one: the toolbar, undo, images and ⌘T act on it.
- Drag the line between two columns to resize them (double-click it to even them out). Only the columns resize; the sidebar does not. With many columns open, they scroll horizontally.
- Each column has a close button. Closing only hides the column; the sidebar still lists the version, marked closed, and clicking it opens the column again.
- A draft with more than one version can show its versions under its row in the sidebar. Clicking another draft opens it with its versions showing, and every other draft keeps its list as it was; clicking the draft you are in shows or hides its versions. Clicking a version of any draft opens that draft at that version. Drafts in the sidebar have no hover fill and keep the normal arrow cursor; only the open draft is highlighted. The version list is compact, with each version's last-edited time at the end of its line; it has no guide line, background or hover fill, and its names line up with the draft's own text; the open draft's highlight wraps both its row and its versions, and the version you are in is shown in the full text colour. Each draft remembers which version you were in.
- **⌘1–⌘8** open that draft in the sidebar and **⌘9** the last one. Shortcut labels are not shown in the sidebar.
- **Right-click** a draft in the sidebar for: Open, Rename (inline), New version, Pin / Unpin, open any of its versions, set its status, Export, Archive, and Delete draft. Right-click a version (its tab or its row in the sidebar) for: Rename (typed right in its tab; clearing the name goes back to the first sentence, and naming never moves the draft up the sidebar), New version (copied from it), Close tab, and Delete version. Deleting asks first and moves the draft or version into Draft's Recovery folder.
- Every version stays editable and saves continuously. Starting a new version does not freeze earlier ones.
- Deleting a version is a separate manual action with confirmation; published versions cannot be deleted.
- In lists and search, a draft is matched by the text of all its versions. The inbox shows the newest version's title and preview.
- Publishing is always recorded against a specific version, so the published text stays traceable even if that version is later edited. Record a snapshot of the text at the time of publishing.

## Inbox

- When the window is wide enough, an inbox sidebar lists every draft. Narrow windows hide it behind a toggle. **⌘B** shows and hides the sidebar from anywhere (bold is in the right-click menu).
- Each row shows the title on one line with the last-edited time in its top right corner; a title too long to fit is cut off and scrolls on a loop while you hover it. Below it: a preview, then the stage and the platforms it is live on, with the number of versions in the lower right corner.
- The preview is the draft's opening sentences until the connected AI has written a one-sentence summary, which then replaces it. Summaries are written once a draft has about 25 words, after you pause, one draft at a time, and again when the newest version changes by a fair amount (about a fifth, at least 25 words).
- Right-clicking empty space in the sidebar offers New draft, Import Markdown or text file, and Restore last archived draft.
- Sorted by last edited, with pinned drafts first (separated from the rest by a line, no icon). Only writing counts as an edit: opening, closing, adding or deleting versions, renaming, AI titles and summaries, pinning and changing status never move a draft. Filter by stage and by platform.

## Progress

Each draft moves through four stages:

| Stage | How it gets there |
| --- | --- |
| Idea | Automatic: fewer than about 50 words in the newest version and only one version. |
| Drafting | Automatic: anything past Idea that isn't Live. |
| Ready | Manual: you set it. |
| Live | Automatic: at least one publication link is recorded. |

The status shows only in the sidebar, not in the writing area. Clicking a draft's status there opens a small menu showing just the four status pills (no checkmarks or toggles); click one to switch. Choosing the stage Draft would work out anyway keeps the draft automatic (so publishing can still make it Live); choosing any other stage sets it by hand until you change it. The writing area has no undo, redo or Markdown buttons; ⌘Z and ⇧⌘Z still work. The inbox and draft header also show how much a draft has been iterated: version count and when it was last edited.

## Socials and analytics

- Preferences has a profile where you add your handles for Instagram, TikTok, X, Threads, and YouTube.
- From any version, **Mark as published** records the platform, the post URL, the date, and the version.
- Draft reads public metrics (views, likes, comments, shares, where available) from the post page without official APIs. Metrics refresh automatically once a day while Draft is running, and on demand.
- A draft shows each publication with its metrics history. A simple comparison across published drafts shows which posts did best.
- Scraping can break when a platform changes or blocks requests. A failed refresh keeps the last known numbers, shows when they were last updated, and never blocks writing. An explicit, per-platform browser-session opt-in (as toscript already supports) may be used for posts that need sign-in.

## References

- Every link, image, and video in any version of any draft is collected into **References**. Filter by type (links, images, videos) and search across them.
- A reference shows which drafts use it and opens the draft it came from.
- **A draft that is only a link** is a link draft: Draft automatically pulls its full text (articles) or transcript (YouTube, X, Instagram, TikTok video) and stores it with the draft. It is searchable and available to AI questions.
- A link inside a larger draft is collected into References but its transcript is not pulled automatically. It can be pulled on demand from the reference or the link preview.

### Transcripts (toscript, built in)

toscript moves into this repository and ships inside Draft. Users do not install it, clone it, or open a terminal.

- Source: the toscript Python package lives under `transcribe/` in this repo, keeping its tests.
- Runtime: Draft bundles `uv`, FFmpeg, and Deno. On the first transcript request Draft prepares an isolated Python environment from the locked dependencies.
- Captions are used when available. Otherwise audio is transcribed locally with MLX Whisper (Apple Silicon only). The model (about 1.6 GB) downloads on the first transcription that needs it, with visible progress and the option to cancel.
- Transcripts are stored with the draft, with timestamps.
- yt-dlp from this runtime is also used to read public post metrics where it supports the platform.
- Article full text is extracted locally from the page (readable text, title, author, date).

## Search and asking

- **⌘K** opens global search across every version of every draft, link-draft transcripts and article text, and titles. Results are grouped by draft and show which version matched.
- From the same box, **Ask** turns the query into a question for the connected AI. Draft retrieves the most relevant drafts, versions, and transcripts locally and sends only those as context. Answers cite the drafts they used, and citations open them.
- Search works without an AI connection; Ask requires one.

## Files

Each draft is a macOS package: a folder that Finder shows and moves as a single file.

```
~/Library/Application Support/Draft/Drafts/
  <id>.draft/
    draft.json          title, title source, stage override, versions, publications, links, timestamps
    versions/
      v1.md
      v2.md
    history/            undo and redo history for each version
    media/              images (original + crops) and videos
    references/         pulled transcripts and article text
```

- Version text is plain Markdown. Media is referenced by relative path, so the package is readable without Draft.
- `draft.json` carries an explicit `schemaVersion`. Unknown fields are preserved.
- **Export** writes a plain folder (or a zip) with the same layout plus clean `.md` and `.txt` copies of each version, ready for a video editor or another tool.
- A search index lives separately and is rebuilt from the packages; it is never the source of truth.
- Existing `.draft.json` documents migrate on first launch of 2.0: the current text becomes v1, images move into `media/`, and the original files are kept in a recovery folder.

### Syncing with Zo

Settings → Sync keeps the `Drafts/` packages in sync with a Zo Knowledge folder the person chooses, both ways. The folder mirrors each package: `<id>.draft/` with `draft.json`, `versions/v<n>.md` and `media/`, and `Archive/<id>.draft/` for archived drafts. `history/` stays on each Mac. `Drafts.md` at the top lists each live draft's title and folder. Without a connection Draft is local only.

- **Browser sign-in.** Draft uses the registered public client `draft-desktop`, PKCE S256, and an ephemeral loopback callback at `http://127.0.0.1:<port>/callback`. The desktop host verifies state and issuer, exchanges the code, and stores the refresh token in Keychain. Refreshes are serialized across processes sharing a data folder. A persisted uncertainty marker prevents replay after a process stops during rotation. Sign-ins last at most 30 days. Disconnect revokes the sign-in before removing local credentials. Legacy `.env` connections remain supported separately.
- **Connect, then choose.** The person chooses an identity on Zo's consent page. Draft recommends a dedicated service account with workspace Builder and folder-scoped Knowledge: all. The optional setup prompt creates these resources without issuing secrets or falling back to Full access. Consent grants no extra permissions. `GET /session` supplies authorized workspace names and IDs, and every OAuth invocation sends the selected `Zo-Workspace-Id`. The person then chooses a folder through a paginated `zo.kb.list` picker; nothing uploads beforehand. Changing the folder preserves the old folder's copy. Reconnect lets the person select a workspace and folder again.
- **Two-way, local working copy.** Each ordinary check reads the metadata of `Draft-sync.json` using a workspace-qualified GET and an ETag. An unchanged index skips cloud folder inventories. Bootstrap, index changes, local changes, interrupted publications, manual checks, and periodic audits use `zo.kb.list` with `details: true`, following every page and only descending into draft packages, Archive, versions and media. Incomplete inventories fail without reconciling deletions. Full scans clear the folder cache; scans for local changes can use overlapping timestamp filters in version/media folders. MD5 matches acknowledge metadata-only revisions without downloading content. Per draft:
  - **Changed on one side only:** that side wins.
  - **Changed on both sides:** merged by `draft-merge.ts`. Versions changed on one side are taken from it. A version changed on both keeps this Mac's text, and Zo's becomes a new version "(from Zo)". Added and removed versions are judged against `baseVersions`, the version IDs both sides last agreed on. Draft details come from whichever side changed them, this Mac first.
  - **Downloads:** each file comes through `zo.file.read` with `format: url`.
  - **Writes go through `DraftStore`:** `writeExternal`, `moveExternal` and `removeExternal`. They queue behind the window's saves and are noted in a change feed.
- **The window.** The window polls `GET /api/library/changes?since=` every 2 seconds and takes in changed, new, archived and deleted drafts, unless a draft has unsaved typing.
  - Each save sends `seen`, the last sync change the window took in for that draft. `DraftStore.save` keeps the pre-pull text and metadata of each pulled draft. A save that started before a pull is merged against it and returned, and the window adopts the result while keeping anything typed since.
  - The editor applies outside changes as minimal edits.
- **Deletes and moves.**
  - **Deleted here:** `Recovery/Deleted/<time>-<id>.draft` marks it. Its files in Zo are deleted with `zo.file.delete` and then `zo.file.purge`. The emptied folders are queued in `cleanup` and removed with `zo.folder.delete` once Zo allows; a folder holding trashed files can't be deleted until the purge job finishes. The ID is kept in `deleted`, so search lag can't bring the draft back unless it was edited in Zo after the delete.
  - **Deleted in Zo:** a draft missing from the search is confirmed with `zo.file.get_metadata` on its known file IDs before it moves to Recovery here. A draft written in here since is kept and uploaded again. If more than two drafts and more than a third of known drafts disappear in one pass, nothing is removed and Settings says so.
  - **Archive and restore:** these move the package folder with `zo.folder.update` (`destination_folder_id`), keeping file IDs. A move seen on the other side moves the local package.
- **Shared index.** `Draft-sync.json` contains a generation and up to 128 recent draft change tokens, including archive/delete hints. It is an invalidation signal, never authority to delete writing. A pending announcement is saved locally before each cloud mutation and cleared only after the index is published. Index replacement uses `expected_revision`; conflicts reread and merge other writers' entries. A publication based on an index newer than the inventory triggers another pass so a concurrent writer's changes cannot be acknowledged without being read. A failed publication survives restart. No hostagent changes, event subscriptions, or extra cloud service are required.
- **When.** Sync runs in the local server after a save, once there has been a 15-second pause, at most 2 minutes after the first unsaved change. Startup, foregrounding, and network reconnection check immediately, with a 15-second throttle for repeated activity events. Scheduled checks run every 2 minutes while active; after 5 minutes without activity, or while in the background, they back off to 15 minutes. A background check does not interrupt the writing debounce. A complete inventory at least every 30 minutes catches older clients and direct cloud edits; **Sync now** forces one immediately. Blank drafts that were never synced stay on their Mac. `authorization_changed` is retried like other transient errors.
- **Uploads.** Files go up by path with `zo.file.create` (`create_parent_folders`) or `zo.file.update` with `expected_revision`. Images and text over 512 KB go through `zo.file.prepare_create` or `prepare_update` and a PUT to the returned storage URL. Deterministic idempotency keys make transient failures safe to retry. A revision conflict leaves remote text intact and waits for the next reconciliation. A file appearing after inventory also waits for reconciliation. A missing version file is created again from this Mac's copy. `zo-sync.json` records each file's ID, revision, SHA-256, MD5, size and modification time.

## Removed

Removed entirely, along with their data fields, settings, tests, and catalog entries:

- Alternate wording (word, sentence, and paragraph alternatives, with their markers and tones)
- Set aside / revive (ghosted passages)
- Overflow panel
- English article cycling
- The Lab (trim previews, tone and clarity review)

Migration keeps the text as it currently reads: the selected wording of each alternative stays, ghosted passages are kept as ordinary text, and overflow text is appended under an "Overflow" heading so nothing is lost.

Also removed: the original video transcript under `sources/`, `provenance.json`, `SHA256SUMS`, `NOTICE.md`, the October 22 and October 26 specs, and every reference to the original inspiration in the app, README, and website.

## Website

The landing page is casual and about the workflow Draft is built for, not a pitch or an introduction: the headline opens with "Write messy. Shape it later." and the footer is just @erikinfour and GitHub, centered. Under the headline, a looping walkthrough runs the real 2.0 app in a window frame, against an in-memory stand-in for the local server, on a soft desktop background so the glass shows. A glass cursor and on-screen shortcut chips show what is happening, and each scene changes the headline:

- **Start with a brain dump.** ⌘N opens a new draft and a messy, lowercase brain dump is typed out, starting "rough idea first". Its first sentence names it, then the AI title and summary appear in the sidebar (canned answers; nothing leaves the page).
- **Spin off new versions.** ⌘T opens a copy beside it, which is selected and rewritten clean and named "Clean" from the tab's right-click menu; ⌘T again makes a short version from that, named "Short".
- **Refine a few side by side.** Pressing the Clean tab and then the Short tab, a line is added to each.
- **Mark the one that's ready.** The draft's status chip in the sidebar is set to Ready.
- **Add whatever you need.** The gear opens Settings at Make it yours, which starts straight at the request box; "Show a daily word goal in my tabs" is typed in, added to the list and downloaded, then Restart to update remounts the app (the stand-in builds nothing; it adds a Word goal feature). Back in writing, under **Ask for anything. Draft builds it.**, the tab you are in gains one more glass bubble counting its words toward 300, and the headline ends on "Try it on your Mac."

It starts from four sample drafts covering every status, one of them open in two versions. Visitors can pause and replay it; it pauses when hidden or scrolled away, and respects reduced motion. The old parity checks against a scripted feature list are not coming back. The download button is built like an app tab: a "Download for Mac" bubble joined by a neck to a download-icon bubble, in the same gray as a tab. It points at the newest published release.

## Build order

1. **Groundwork:** remove old features and references; new draft package format with migration; ⌘N; ⌥. global shortcut; always running with menu bar icon; titles; reading time; inbox sidebar.
2. **Versions:** version columns, new-version options, horizontal scrolling, close/reopen.
3. **Media and dragging:** video paste and playback, link previews, block dragging with a drop indicator.
4. **References:** link collection and filters, link drafts, toscript built in, article extraction.
5. **Search and Ask.**
6. **Socials:** profile, mark as published, daily metrics refresh, progress stages, comparison.

Each step ships as a normal Draft release.
